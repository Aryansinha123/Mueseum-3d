## Description

Discovery was the third Space Shuttle orbiter vehicle to fly in space.
It entered service in 1984 and became the oldest and most utilized
orbiter in the Space Shuttle fleet.

Discovery flew 39 Earth-orbital missions and spent a total of 365 days
in space. During its operational history, it traveled almost 240 million
kilometers (150 million miles), more than any other Space Shuttle
orbiter.

The orbiter carried 184 men and women into space and back, with some
crew members flying more than once. The total number of crew assignments
was 251.

Because Discovery performed every type of mission that the Space Shuttle
was intended to perform, it represents approximately 30 years of U.S.
human spaceflight history from 1981 to 2011.

Discovery was named after renowned sailing ships of exploration. It has
been preserved as intact as possible following its final flight in 2011.

---

## Historical Significance

Space Shuttle Discovery represents a major period in the history of
human spaceflight and the U.S. Space Shuttle program.

Its 39 Earth-orbital missions, extensive time in space, and wide range
of mission types make it an important artifact for understanding the
development of reusable crewed spacecraft.

Discovery provides a useful example for studying:

- Human spaceflight
- Reusable spacecraft
- Space Shuttle technology
- Earth-orbital missions
- U.S. space exploration history
- Aerospace engineering

## Transfer to the Smithsonian

NASA transferred Discovery to the Smithsonian in April 2012 following
a delivery flight over the nation's capital.

The spacecraft is preserved at the Smithsonian's National Air and Space
Museum.

## 3D Model Information

The 3D model used in this project is the Smithsonian 3D model of
Space Shuttle Discovery.

For the web-based virtual museum and WebXR AR application, the
preferred model format is:

**Low-resolution 3D mesh, GLB, scale in meters**

### Why GLB is used

GLB is suitable for the project because:

- It is directly supported by Three.js.
- It can be loaded using GLTFLoader.
- It packages the model and associated resources into a single file.
- The low-resolution version provides better real-time performance.
- Meter-based scale is suitable for AR spatial placement.
