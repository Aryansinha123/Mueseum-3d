## 2. Historical Significance

The Bell X-1 was the first airplane to fly faster than the speed of sound.

On October 14, 1947, U.S. Air Force Captain Charles E. "Chuck" Yeager piloted the aircraft to a speed of approximately:

- **Speed:** 1,127 km/h (700 mph)
- **Speed:** Mach 1.06
- **Altitude:** 13,000 m (43,000 ft)

Yeager named the aircraft **"Glamorous Glennis"** in tribute to his wife.

The flight demonstrated that aircraft could be designed to fly faster than sound and helped establish practical supersonic flight.

---

## 3. Key Accomplishment

**Broke the Sound Barrier**

The X-1 proved that a manned aircraft could travel faster than the speed of sound and gathered important transonic flight data.

---

## 4. Development

The XS-1/X-1 program was developed through a cooperative program initiated in 1944 by:

- National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA)
- U.S. Army Air Forces

Bell Aircraft Corporation was contracted in 1945 to develop three transonic and supersonic research aircraft.

The aircraft was powered by a rocket engine and was air-launched from Boeing B-29 or B-50 Superfortress aircraft.

---

## 5. Aircraft Design

The Bell X-1 was a:

- Single-engine aircraft
- Single-seat aircraft
- Mid-wing rocket plane
- International-orange painted aircraft

Its smooth fuselage was based on the aerodynamic shape of a **.50-caliber machine-gun bullet**.

The fuselage contained:

- Two propellant tanks
- Twelve nitrogen spheres
- Pressurized cockpit
- Pressure regulators
- Retractable landing gear
- Wing carry-through structure
- Rocket engine
- Flight-test instrumentation

The aircraft used a **Reaction Motors, Inc. rocket engine** producing approximately **6,000 pounds of thrust**.

---

## 6. Flight History

The Bell X-1 completed a total of **78 flights**.

On March 26, 1948, Chuck Yeager reached:

- **Speed:** 1,540 km/h (957 mph)
- **Speed:** Mach 1.45
- **Altitude:** approximately 21,900 m (71,900 ft)

This represented the highest velocity and altitude reached by a manned airplane up to that time.

The X-1 continued flight-test operations until mid-1950.

It completed:

- 19 contractor demonstration flights
- 59 Air Force test flights

---

## 7. Impact and Innovation

The Bell X-1 demonstrated that the so-called **"sound barrier"** could be overcome by a manned aircraft.

Its flight-test program provided valuable transonic and supersonic flight data and contributed to the development of high-speed aircraft.

The X-1 therefore represents an important milestone in the transition from subsonic to supersonic aviation.
