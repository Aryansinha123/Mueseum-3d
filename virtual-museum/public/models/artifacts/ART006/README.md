## Description

The Apollo 11 Command Module, "Columbia," served as the living quarters
for the three-person crew during most of the first crewed lunar landing
mission in July 1969.

On July 16, 1969, Neil Armstrong, Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin, and Michael
Collins were launched from Cape Kennedy aboard a Saturn V rocket.

The Command Module was one of three major parts of the Apollo spacecraft.
The other two parts were:

- Service Module
- Lunar Module "Eagle"

The Service Module contained the main spacecraft propulsion system and
consumables.

The Lunar Module "Eagle" was the two-person spacecraft used by Neil
Armstrong and Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin to descend to the Moon's surface on
July 20, 1969.

The Command Module was the only portion of the Apollo spacecraft that
returned to Earth.

---

## Historical Significance

Apollo 11 "Columbia" is one of the most significant artifacts in the
history of human spaceflight.

It was used during the first crewed lunar landing mission in July 1969
and carried Neil Armstrong, Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin, and Michael Collins to
the Moon and back.

The Apollo Command Module represents a major milestone in human
spaceflight and the technological development required to conduct
crewed lunar missions.

The Apollo CM Columbia has been designated a:

**"Milestone of Flight"**

by the National Air and Space Museum.

---

## Apollo 11 Crew

The Command Module carried three astronauts:

- **Neil A. Armstrong**
- **Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin**
- **Michael Collins**

The crew launched on July 16, 1969.

Armstrong and Aldrin used the Lunar Module "Eagle" to descend to the
Moon's surface on July 20, 1969, while Columbia remained in lunar orbit
with Michael Collins.
